# Reward Biases Spontaneous Neural Reactivation During Sleep

**Dataset**: `openneuro_ds003574` | **Year**: 2021 | **URL**: https://openneuro.org/datasets/ds003574
**Category**: Complex Applied | **Subjects**: 18 | **Files (Completed)**: 17 | **Channels**: 69 | **Sampling Rate**: 500 Hz | **Manual Review**: Pending

## Paradigm

Participants played two video games (FACE and MAZE) during a waking session—one rewarded (win condition) and one non-rewarded (lose condition)—followed by a nap sleep session recorded with simultaneous EEG-fMRI. The study investigates whether reward enhances spontaneous neural reactivation of game-related memory traces during sleep. Brain patterns from waking gameplay are decoded and compared against sleep activity to identify replay events. The dataset contains BrainVision numeric S-code triggers from the game paradigm with varying per-file occurrence depending on subject completion.
